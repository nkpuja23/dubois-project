Integrating the Archive with Du Bois

Rather than simply observing history, Goin's letter forces us to see Du Bois's sociological theories in active practice. She represents the exact kind of professional ambition that challenged the racial boundaries of the 1930s (Goin, 1933).

Of our Spiritual Strivings

Goin's simultaneous presence in an elite white institution and her search for Black hospitals perfectly illustrates the "two unreconciled strivings" (Du Bois, 2018, pp. 1-12). She was basically living out this double consciousness, seeking to merge her Yale education with her dedication to the Black community without compromising her identity.

Of Mr. Booker T. Washington and Others

Du Bois explicitly criticized Washington because his agenda "depreciates institutions of higher learning" (Du Bois, 2018, p. 42) in favor of industrial work. Goin’s letter proves that Black professionals were refusing this compromise, pushing past vocational limits to claim space in elite, specialized academic arenas (Du Bois, 2018, pp. 40-57).

The Souls of White Folk

Even after gaining access to Yale, Goin had to search for separate Black hospitals to report on and potentially work in. This reflects Du Bois's critique of systemic exclusion, demonstrating that academic admission did not erase the overarching "color line" present in white-dominated professions (Du Bois, 2007).

Decrypting the Striving

Complete the phrase to unlock Goin's motivation.

"It is a peculiar sensation, this ___________, this sense of always looking at one’s self through the eyes of others..."

Correct. Goin operated within Yale (a white institution) while seeking Black hospitals, perfectly embodying this dual existence (Du Bois, 2018)[cite: 9].
