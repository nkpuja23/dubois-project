Integrating the Archive with Du Bois

Goin's letter illustrates how Black professionals pursued higher education while remaining devoted to community uplift, bringing up questions about how Black women in STEM navigated the dual expectations of white-dominated institutions and their own communities[cite: 1].

Of our Spiritual Strivings[cite: 1, 4]

This highlights the "double consciousness" and the striving for education as a means to lift the Veil, matching Goin's ambitions[cite: 1].

Of Mr. Booker T. Washington and Others[cite: 1, 4]

This refutes Washington's limitation to industrial training; Goin's pursuit of an advanced nursing degree embodies Du Bois’s advocacy for higher, specialized education[cite: 1].

The Souls of White Folk[cite: 1, 4]

This provides context on the systemic white supremacy within institutions that Goin had to navigate, contrasting her community-focused research with the exclusivity of white academia[cite: 1].
