The Archival Artifact

On October 16, 1933, Viola B. Goin, the first Black student at the Yale School of Nursing, wrote a letter to The Crisis requesting information on Black hospitals across the country.

This physical artifact serves as a tangible intersection of race, higher education, and institutional access during the early 20th century. View Original Document in CREDO Archive →

Double Consciousness in Action

Goin's letter encapsulates the "double consciousness" theorized in Of our Spiritual Strivings. Operating within an elite, white-dominated space (Yale), her inquiry demonstrates a profound commitment to her own community by actively seeking Black medical institutions.

Defying the Washington Compromise

In Of Mr. Booker T. Washington and Others, Du Bois fiercely critiques the limitation of Black education to industrial training. Goin’s pursuit of a highly specialized nursing degree at an Ivy League institution is a living manifestation of Du Bois’s demand for intellectual and professional advancement.

Navigating Systemic Supremacy

Goin's very presence at Yale, and her subsequent need to seek out independent Black hospitals, provides stark context to the environment detailed in The Souls of White Folk. The systemic exclusivity of white institutions necessitated the parallel development of Black professional networks.
