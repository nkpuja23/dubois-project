The Archival Artifact

On October 16, 1933, Viola B. Goin, the first Black student at the Yale School of Nursing, wrote a letter to The Crisis requesting information on Black hospitals across the country.

This physical artifact serves as a tangible intersection of race, higher education, and institutional access. View Original Document →

The Arc of Ambition

1903

The Souls of Black Folk Published: Du Bois introduces "double consciousness" and fiercely critiques Booker T. Washington's industrial compromise.

1920

Darkwater Published: The Souls of White Folk dissects the systemic exclusivity and racial superiority built into American institutions.

1933

The Goin Letter: Viola B. Goin, pushing past industrial limitations into Ivy League academia, writes to The Crisis, actively navigating the very systems Du Bois critiqued.
